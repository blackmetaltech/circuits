Chapter 3 · Charge

Wren's third page: four jobs at Stall 14, Lantern Lane, and this is where time comes in. Time a camera flash with a capacitor, calm a bouncing arcade button, speed up a relay with a bigger supply, then sit the capacitor-bank exam.

3-1 · Flash Timer classic look

Ines runs the camera shop. The flash's ready-light must come on 1 second after power-on. Build a timer from a resistor and an electrolytic capacitor: the resistor from IN to OUT, the capacitor from OUT to GND. The ready-light turns on when OUT reaches 6.32 V, which is 63.2 % of the 10 V supply, and the ticket wants that at 1.00 s, give or take 5 %. Wren: a capacitor charges through the resistor, and how long it takes depends on R times C. Watch the curve form on the scope.

The bench checks

  • OUT crosses 6.32 V (63 % of the 10 V supply) between 0.95 s and 1.05 s after power-on, with the scope probe on it
  • The whole curve stays inside the amber tube around 10 V x (1 - e^(-t / 1 s))

Parts tray: Carbon-film resistor 5 %, Electrolytic capacitor

3-2 · Debounce classic look

Dex repairs arcade machines. The big button's contact bounces: for about 2 ms after every press, and every release, it chatters open and shut, and the game reads a burst of presses. The logic input only asks whether OUT is above or below 2.5 V. Put a resistor from SW to OUT and a capacitor from OUT to GND, so OUT crosses 2.5 V exactly once on the press and once on the release, 5 to 20 ms after the contact first touches. The 10 kilohm pull-up on the board takes part in the release. Wren: a low-pass filter turns a burst of edges into one slow slope.

The bench checks

  • OUT falls through 2.5 V exactly once on the press (no re-crossings)
  • OUT rises through 2.5 V exactly once on the release (no re-crossings)
  • On the press OUT falls through 2.5 V 5 to 20 ms after the button first touches, in every bounce pattern
  • On the release OUT rises through 2.5 V 5 to 20 ms after the button first lifts, in every bounce pattern

Parts tray: Carbon-film resistor 5 %, Ceramic capacitor, Electrolytic capacitor, 1N4148 signal diode

3-3 · Relay Pull-in classic look

Sami's greenhouse irrigation controller needs its relay to pull in within 0.4 ms of switch-on, or the valves drift out of step. The relay (soldered on the board) has a 200 ohm coil with 100 millihenry of inductance, pulls in at 40 mA and is rated for 60 mA. The board has a 12 V rail and a 24 V rail: wire one of them to the COIL pad, with or without a resistor. Wren: a coil's current rises the way a capacitor's voltage does, with a time constant of L over R. Watch the current on the scope.

The bench checks

  • The coil current reaches the 40 mA pull-in within 0.40 ms of switch-on
  • The steady coil current is between 50 mA and 66 mA (the coil is rated 60 mA), and no part is over its rating

Parts tray: Carbon-film resistor 5 %

3-4 · Exam: Capacitor Bank examclassic look

Card (a): a 2 µF capacitor in series with a 3 µF and a 6 µF in parallel, across 110 V. Find the charge and the total energy stored. Card (b): a 4 µF capacitor charged to 100 V is connected to an uncharged 6 µF. Find the final voltage and the energy lost. Predict first, then build both cards on the board and let the bench measure them.

The bench checks

  • Card (a): charge on the bank (µC)
  • Card (a): total energy stored (mJ)
  • Card (b): final voltage (V)
  • Card (b): energy lost (mJ)
  • Card (a): the point M between the 2 µF and the pair sits at 20.0 V (the 2 µF carries the whole charge, 90 V across it)
  • Card (b): the shared voltage on D is 40.0 V
  • Card (b): the heat in the switch is 12.0 mJ, the energy ledger

Parts tray: Exact capacitor
