Chapter 5 · Switches & Gain

Wren's fifth page: five jobs from the maker space on Lantern Lane. A pin runs a fan, a 3.3 V board lights a strip, loose parts become a NAND gate, a synth gets a preamp, and the exam desk asks about gain.

5-1 · Fan Driver new look

Kit's maker space has a microcontroller whose pin gives 5 V through 25 ohm and must never give more than 20 mA here. It has to switch a 12 V fan (60 ohm and a 10 mH winding: 200 mA). A pin cannot do that alone, so a transistor does the heavy lifting: the pin gives a small base current and the transistor lets a hundred times more through the fan. The fan must see at least 11.0 V with the pin high and at most 0.5 V with it low, and the bench also switches it on and off at 100 Hz.

Arrives: Q1 is burnt, and nothing across the fan catches its turn-off kick.

The bench checks

  • The fan gets at least 11.0 V when the pin is high
  • The fan gets at most 0.5 V when the pin is low
  • The pin gives at most 20 mA when it is high

Parts tray: 2N2222A NPN, BC547B NPN, TIP120, 1N4007 rectifier diode, 1N4148 signal diode, Carbon-film resistor 5 %

5-2 · 3.3 V Gate classic look

Tess's board speaks 3.3 V and a 12 V LED strip (40 ohm, 0.3 A) hangs on it. A MOSFET is a switch you drive with voltage, not current: it conducts when the gate sits far enough above its threshold. The strip must get at least 0.28 A, the FET must stay under 125 C at 40 C, and the bench repeats the test with the threshold at its datasheet minimum, typical and maximum, because no two batches agree. A fourth test lets the pin float while the board resets: the strip must stay off.

The bench checks

  • The strip gets at least 0.28 A from 3.3 V, with the threshold at minimum, typical and maximum
  • The strip stays under 50 mA while the pin floats at 1.8 V (reset)

Parts tray: 2N7000 FET, IRLZ44N FET, IRF540N FET, Carbon-film resistor 5 %

5-3 · Gate Builder classic look

Dev's robotics club has no logic chips left, only diodes, a transistor and a drawer of resistors. Build a NAND gate: the output Y is low only when both inputs A and B are high. The bench drives A and B with a real logic low (0.4 V, not 0 V) or a high (5 V) and tries all four combinations. When the output should be high it must hold at least 4.0 V while a 10 kilohm load pulls on it (a fan-out of a few gates); when it should be low it must hold at most 0.4 V while sinking 1 mA.

The bench checks

  • With the output high (any input low) Y holds at least 4.0 V into a 10 kilohm load
  • With both inputs high Y holds at most 0.4 V while sinking 1 mA

Parts tray: 1N4148 signal diode, 2N3904 NPN, Carbon-film resistor 5 %

5-4 · Synth Preamp classic look

Juno's music shop has a synth whose line-out gives 100 mV peak at 1 kHz through 600 ohm. The amplifier behind it wants about ten times that, with one transistor from a 9 V supply. Build a common-emitter stage: a divider that sets the bias, a collector resistor, a small emitter resistor that fixes the gain, and a coupling capacitor at the input and the output so only the signal passes. The gain from the source must be between 8.5 and 11 with at most 5 % distortion, whatever the transistor's gain (the bench tries 100, 200 and 300).

The bench checks

  • The output swing is 1.7 to 2.2 V peak to peak (a gain of 8.5 to 11 from the 0.1 V peak source), for transistor gains of 100, 200 and 300
  • The distortion of the output is at most 5 %

Parts tray: 2N3904 NPN, BC547B NPN, Carbon-film resistor 5 %, Electrolytic capacitor

5-5 · Exam: CE Amplifier examclassic look

Exam card: VCC = 12 V, RB = 565 kilohm from VCC to the base, RC = 3 kilohm from VCC to the collector, beta = 100, VBE = 0.7 V. Find IB, IC, VCE and the voltage gain. Predict first, then build it and measure.

The bench checks

  • IB, the base current (microamps)
  • IC, the collector current (milliamps)
  • VCE, the collector-emitter voltage (volts)
  • Av, the voltage gain, with its sign
  • The collector current agrees with the textbook answer, give or take 1 %
  • The collector voltage agrees with the textbook answer, give or take 1 %

Parts tray: Exact resistor, Textbook NPN
